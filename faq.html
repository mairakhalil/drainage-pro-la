Drainage Problems

Standing water often develops because of low spots, compacted soil, poor grading, blocked drainage paths, or runoff entering faster than the property can drain it.

Water may collect near a foundation when surrounding soil slopes toward the home, downspouts discharge nearby, or surface runoff lacks an effective path away.

Common signs include persistent puddles, soggy landscaping, soil erosion, runoff crossing walkways, water near the foundation, and areas that remain wet after rainfall.

Yes. Concentrated runoff can carry soil away, create channels, expose landscaping roots, and gradually change the grade or stability of affected areas.

Slow-draining soil, low elevations, poor grading, excessive irrigation, or inadequate drainage can leave sections of a yard saturated well after rainfall ends.

Drainage Solutions

The appropriate solution depends on where water originates, how it travels, property slope, soil conditions, problem areas, and where collected water can discharge appropriately.

A French drain collects subsurface and surrounding water along a drainage path, while a catch basin captures concentrated surface water at a specific low point.

Channel and trench drains are commonly considered where surface water crosses driveways, patios, walkways, garage entrances, or other hardscape areas requiring linear collection.

Regrading can improve drainage when improper elevations cause water to collect or move toward unwanted areas. Some properties may also require additional drainage components.

Hillside drainage may use strategically placed drains, collection points, piping, grading, or other runoff controls based on slope, water movement, and site conditions.
