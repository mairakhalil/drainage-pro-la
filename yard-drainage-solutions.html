Yard drainage solutions address standing water, soggy lawn areas, recurring puddles, and runoff that does not drain properly after rainfall. Our contractor network evaluates yard elevations, soil conditions, low spots, hardscape runoff, and existing drainage to identify the source of the problem.

The right solution depends on how water enters and moves through the property. Drainage improvements may involve surface drains, underground piping, French drains, catch basins, or targeted grading to collect, redirect, or improve the movement of unwanted water.

Professional Yard Drainage Solutions

Effective yard drainage starts with identifying why water is collecting rather than simply treating the visible puddle. Contractors represented by Drainage Pro LA assess drainage patterns, landscape elevations, soil saturation, runoff sources, and suitable discharge routes before recommending an approach.

Each property can require a different drainage strategy. A low lawn may benefit from surface collection, while persistent subsurface moisture may require a French drain. Properties receiving concentrated runoff may need multiple drainage components working together as one coordinated system.

Common Yard Drainage Problems

Drainage problems often become noticeable after heavy rain when water remains in the yard instead of moving toward an appropriate outlet. Low spots, compacted soil, poor grading, concentrated roof runoff, and surrounding hardscape can all contribute to recurring wet areas.

A professional assessment helps determine whether the problem is primarily surface runoff, subsurface moisture, grading, or a combination of conditions. Licensed contractors within our network can recommend drainage improvements based on the property's actual water patterns and site conditions.

Yard Drainage FAQs

Find answers about standing water, soggy yards, runoff, drainage options, and other important considerations when improving residential yard drainage in Los Angeles.

Standing water can result from low spots, poor grading, compacted soil, concentrated runoff, or inadequate drainage. Evaluating where the water originates and how it moves helps determine the appropriate drainage solution.

The best solution depends on the cause of the moisture. French drains, catch basins, surface drains, underground piping, or grading improvements may be recommended based on soil, elevations, runoff, and drainage conditions.

French drains generally address water moving through saturated soil, while catch basins collect concentrated surface runoff. Some properties benefit from combining drainage methods when multiple water problems are present.

Properly planned yard drainage can help redirect surface water away from problem areas near a home. The appropriate method depends on grading, runoff sources, drainage routes, and surrounding site conditions.

A drainage evaluation typically considers low points, yard slope, soil conditions, runoff sources, existing drains, hardscape, and potential discharge routes to determine which improvements are appropriate for the property.
